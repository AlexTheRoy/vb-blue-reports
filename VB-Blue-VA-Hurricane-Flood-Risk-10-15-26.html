Every populated Virginia ZIP code scored for hurricane and flooding, with strong wind and tornado as smaller inputs, from the FEMA National Risk Index (December 2025 release) at census-tract resolution, weighted to where each ZIP's residents live, and grouped into four tiers. These are Virginia's two largest property perils in FEMA's own building-loss estimates: flooding, from tidal and storm-surge water around the Chesapeake Bay to river flooding in the mountains, and hurricane wind, which is highest on the Eastern Shore and in Hampton Roads and reaches inland across Southside and into the Piedmont and the Blue Ridge. 03

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • Why these twoFEMA's expected annual building loss for Virginia ranks inland flooding ($1,108 million a year), hurricane ($190 million a year), earthquake ($75 million a year), tornado ($42 million a year). Flooding, inland and coastal together, is 76% of all modeled building loss in the state and hurricane is next; hail ranks seventh.
  • HurricaneNRI hurricane risk: modeled hurricane wind frequency and strength times the value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. Florida and the Gulf Coast set the top of the national scale, and half of Virginia housing sits in ZIPs scoring 69.4 or less, so the cut points are set for Virginia.
  • FloodingNRI inland and coastal flooding risk: each ZIP takes the higher of its two scores, so a tidal ZIP on the Bay and a river ZIP in the mountains are measured on the same 0 to 100 scale. Half of Virginia housing sits in ZIPs scoring 53.3 or less.
  • Supporting inputsNRI strong wind and tornado risk: added to the composite score at lower weight, so ZIPs where several perils add up are caught. Hail is in the spreadsheet only.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (roof age and material, construction, elevation, flood zone, distance to the water) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards. Flood is excluded from a standard home policy and is quoted separately.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeHurricane score 84+ or flooding score 88+
  • HighHurricane score 79+, flooding score 80+, or composite score 70+
  • ModerateHurricane score 71+, flooding score 62+, or composite score 58+
  • LowEverything else.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme is sized to mark about one Virginia home in nine (11.1% of housing). The hurricane arm (84+) covers 6.3% of Virginia housing and the flooding arm (88+) covers 7.7%. High and Moderate widen each arm, and the composite catches ZIPs where several perils add up.

04

Home leads: recommended treatment

Extreme tier gets an appetite check · High tier gets a wind, flood and deductible review

Check appetite · Extreme · 285 ZIPs · 11.1% of VA housing Wind, flood and deductible review · High · 222 ZIPs · 20.4% No change · 379 ZIPs · 68.6%
Excluding every Extreme ZIP would remove 11.1% of Virginia housing from Home; adding the High tier would bring it to 31.4%. We do not recommend a blanket exclusion. We recommend excluding only the ZIPs where your carriers are not writing new home business today, and treating the rest of the Extreme and High tiers as a conversation: roof age and how wind is written, how the hurricane or wind deductible works, and whether the home needs or would benefit from a flood policy.
Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeCheck appetite. Confirm your carriers are writing new home business in the ZIP before the first call. In coastal ZIPs, ask the roof's age, whether the home has storm shutters or impact-rated openings, its elevation and flood zone, and explain how the hurricane deductible works; offer a flood quote. In river-flood ZIPs, ask how close the home sits to the river or creek and whether the lender requires flood, and offer a flood quote.
  • HighWind, flood and deductible review. Ask the year the roof was replaced, walk through the wind or hurricane deductible, often a percentage of the dwelling amount, and ask whether the home has ever taken on water. Offer flood as a follow-up quote.
  • ModerateNo change. Standard quote; a flood question is still worth asking anywhere near the water.
  • LowNo change. Standard quote.
Three kinds of Extreme ZIP
  • Hurricane166 ZIPs reach Extreme on hurricane (score 84+), holding 231,225 housing units: Eastern Shore & Bay Counties 86, Southside 39, Hampton Roads 29, Shenandoah Valley & Central 7, Fredericksburg & Piedmont 5.
  • Coastal flooding138 ZIPs reach Extreme on coastal flooding (score 88+), holding 214,572 housing units: Eastern Shore & Bay Counties 88, Hampton Roads 45, Richmond Region 4, Fredericksburg & Piedmont 1.
  • River flooding78 ZIPs reach Extreme on inland flooding (score 88+), holding 68,548 housing units: Shenandoah Valley & Central 44, Southwest Virginia 25, Roanoke & Lynchburg 7, Hampton Roads 1, Southside 1.
  • Both97 ZIPs meet both the hurricane and the flooding rule.

Hampton Roads

Your largest lead area · with the Richmond area and Northern Virginia

Hampton Roads
The seven cities' 62 ZIPs: 23 Extreme, 28 High, 10 Moderate and 1 Low. Here both perils meet: half of the cities' housing sits in ZIPs scoring 77.4 or more for flooding (in 61 of the 62 ZIPs the coastal score is the higher one: tidal and storm-surge flooding) and 78.9 or more for hurricane. Of its 51 High and Extreme ZIPs, 12 on hurricane, 13 on flooding, 25 on both hurricane and flooding and 1 on the combined score; the largest Extreme ZIPs are 23322 (Chesapeake), 23669 (Hampton), 23703 (Portsmouth) and 23502 (Norfolk). Virginia Beach scores lower than its neighbors: 7 of its 12 ZIPs are Moderate or Low (2 Extreme, 3 High, 6 Moderate and 1 Low); its High and Extreme ZIPs are 23451 (High), 23452 (High), 23454 (High), 23457 (Extreme) and 23461 (Extreme).
Richmond area
Richmond City, Henrico and Chesterfield's 34 ZIPs: 0 Extreme, 2 High, 8 Moderate and 24 Low. Richmond is inland, so its risk is lower: half of the area's housing sits in ZIPs scoring 63.9 or less for hurricane and 30.2 or less for flooding, river flooding along the James. Both of its High and Extreme ZIPs reach that tier on both hurricane and flooding. Northern Virginia is lower still: Fairfax, Prince William and Loudoun's 74 ZIPs are 0 Extreme, 6 High, 24 Moderate and 44 Low, with 1% of housing in High or Extreme ZIPs.

07

Hurricane and flood in the conversation

Questions that make a Virginia home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • Wind84% of housing in Hampton Roads and the Eastern Shore & Bay Counties sits in ZIPs scoring 71+ for hurricane. Roof age and covering, storm shutters and how wind is written today belong in the first conversation, not the renewal.
  • DeductiblesNear the coast, hurricane or wind deductibles are often a percentage of the dwelling amount. Explaining the dollar figure on the first call avoids a surprise after the first storm and builds trust.
  • Tidal flooding45% of Hampton Roads housing sits in ZIPs scoring 80+ for coastal flooding. Flood is excluded from a standard home policy, and many Hampton Roads streets flood at high tide or in a nor'easter without a hurricane at all, so a flood quote is a natural follow-up on every Home lead there, inside or outside a mapped flood zone.
  • River flooding10.6% of Virginians live in tracts FEMA rates high for inland flooding and 1.9% for coastal flooding. In the Shenandoah Valley, Southwest Virginia and along the James, ask how close the home is to the river or creek.
  • AutoFlood and storm damage to a car is paid under comprehensive coverage. On every Auto quote in a High or Extreme ZIP, confirm comprehensive is included; it is also the natural opening to quote the home.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (hurricane, inland flooding, coastal flooding, strong wind, tornado, hail), all 2,186 Virginia tracts. The December 2025 release reports river flooding as inland flooding. Peril choice from the same release's expected annual building loss, summed across Virginia tracts.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside Virginia are not scored. Independent cities are treated as their own county-equivalents. Housing counts from ACS 2020–2024.
  • LeadsAll 1,060 GOAL leads for this account, Jul 22 to Oct 15, 2026 (474 Home, 586 Auto), counted by ZIP. 474 of 474 Home leads and 582 of 586 Auto leads matched a populated Virginia ZIP. Twelve weeks of leads is a modest sample; the tier splits will steady as volume builds.
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a house. Roof age, construction, elevation, flood zone and distance to the water decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesVB-Blue-VA-ZIP-Data-10-15-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and treatment column, plus the demographics used in the companion report.
